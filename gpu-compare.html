What the comparison shows

Pick two to four of the 43 devices LLMScale knows: consumer and workstation cards, data-center GPUs, Apple Silicon and unified-memory AI PCs. Each column is that device's datasheet: architecture, die and process, cores and launch; memory with its type, bus width and bandwidth; dense tensor throughput at BF16, FP8 and FP4; power, form factor, the link between two of them, MIG partitions and the engine that serves models on it.

Each fact comes from the vendor's product page, datasheet or whitepaper where the vendor publishes it, and from a named secondary source where it does not; a value no source settles is left blank rather than guessed. The best value of the memory, bandwidth and tensor rows is highlighted when the columns differ. To see what a model needs on any of these cards, open the VRAM calculator.

Questions

Which of these numbers matters most for running an LLM?
Memory decides what fits: the weights, plus a KV cache for every user's context. Bandwidth decides how fast one user sees tokens, because each token reads every weight once; an RTX 5090 at 1,792 GB/s generates about twice as fast for one user as an L40S at 864 GB/s, although the L40S is rated higher at BF16. Tensor throughput decides how fast prompts are read and how far a large batch scales.
Why is some TFLOPS figure lower here than on the vendor's page?
NVIDIA often prints tensor throughput with 2:4 structured sparsity, which doubles the figure but applies only to weights pruned to that pattern — almost no served LLM is. LLMScale lists the dense rate, halving the sparse one where that is all the vendor prints. GeForce cards are listed at the FP32-accumulate rate, half the FP16-accumulate one.
What does unified memory mean for an LLM?
The CPU and GPU share one pool, so a small device can hold a large model — but the GPU addresses only part of it by default: three-quarters on a Mac from 36 GB up and two-thirds below, 96 of 128 GB on Strix Halo under Windows, nearly all of it on DGX Spark. Their bandwidth is also a fraction of a graphics card's, so they hold big models and run them slowly.
